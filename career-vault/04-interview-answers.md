## Contribution and AI assistance

### Push-back question: “Honestly, using AI for design work feels a bit like cheating.”

#### Intentionally bad answer — keep for contrast

“I don't think using AI for design work is cheating because everyone is using AI these days. I use AI tools to generate design ideas, create multiple screens quickly, and improve my productivity. AI helps me work faster and deliver better designs, so I think designers should learn to use these tools to stay competitive.”

**Why it is weak:** It focuses on tools and speed, offers no evidence of design judgment, and does not explain how the designer validates decisions.

#### Improved STAR answer

**Situation:** At eBitans, a self-service ecommerce website builder for small businesses, customers initially had to upload products one by one.

**Task:** Help make store setup workable for businesses with large product catalogues.

**Action / decision:** Through conversations with customers managing hundreds or even thousands of products, I realized the problem was not a lack of instructions; it was repetitive manual work that did not scale. I helped shape requirements and user-flow direction for a spreadsheet-based bulk-upload solution, discussed feasibility with developers, and collaborated with the team.

**Result / honest proxy:** The team delivered the feature, and customers appreciated the easier upload process. We did not record a verified quantitative impact.

**Spoken answer:**

“I understand that concern. For me, the important distinction is between letting a tool make decisions for me and using tools while remaining accountable for the decisions.

At eBitans, we supported small businesses building their own online stores. Initially, customers had to upload products one by one. Through conversations with customers managing hundreds or even thousands of products, I realized the main problem wasn't a lack of instructions—it was repetitive manual work that didn't scale.

I helped shape the requirements and user-flow direction for a spreadsheet-based bulk upload, discussed feasibility with developers, and worked with the team toward the solution. After launch, customers appreciated the easier upload process, although we didn't establish a quantified impact.

That experience reflects how I approach design: understand the real problem, challenge the initial assumption, evaluate solutions, and use evidence and collaboration to make decisions. I apply the same principle to AI. AI can support exploration, but I remain responsible for validating the output against user needs, constraints, and the intended outcome.”

**Practice check:** Target under 90 seconds when spoken naturally. Rehearse twice, remove any wording that does not sound like your own voice, and keep the distinction between your contribution and the team's delivery clear.

## Decisions and trade-offs

### eBitans: bulk product upload

- **Initial assumption to challenge:** Customers mainly needed more guidance to upload products.
- **Customer insight:** For businesses with large catalogues, one-by-one upload created repetitive work; many customers already had product names, descriptions, and images organized together.
- **Decision direction:** Helped shape a spreadsheet-based bulk-upload flow.
- **Collaboration:** Worked with the team on requirements and user-flow direction and discussed feasibility with developers.
- **Outcome:** Team delivered the feature; customers appreciated the easier upload process. No verified time-saving, conversion, revenue, or launch-speed metric is available.

### eBitans: connected product and inventory management

Customers managing an online store and a physical-shop POS could face duplicated product-management work and inventory inconsistencies. I spoke with customers, helped clarify the problem and requirements, contributed to user-flow and UI direction, and discussed feasibility with developers. The team introduced connected product-management and inventory capability, including stock reduction in the connected POS when a product sold online. Do not claim complete bidirectional syncing or quantified reductions in errors without evidence.
