# Client Proposal Template

**Status:** Reusable template. Complete and verify all project-specific details before sending to a client.

## 1. Purpose

Use this proposal after a prospective client has expressed interest in working together. Its purpose is to confirm our shared understanding of the project, define the scope, establish expectations, and agree on the next steps.

A proposal is not automatically a contract. Formal agreements may be needed depending on the project and jurisdiction.

---

## 2. Pre-Proposal Checklist — Internal Use Only

Before drafting the client-facing proposal, confirm the following:

- [ ] Client or company name and decision-maker
- [ ] Project name and business context
- [ ] Client's problem, needs, and desired outcomes
- [ ] Target audience and relevant user needs
- [ ] Agreed deliverables and acceptance criteria
- [ ] Explicit exclusions and out-of-scope work
- [ ] Required assets, access, content, and client dependencies
- [ ] Project fee, currency, payment schedule, and applicable taxes
- [ ] Estimated schedule and key milestones
- [ ] Feedback, approval, and revision process
- [ ] Ownership, licensing, confidentiality, and portfolio permissions
- [ ] Whether development, implementation, testing, or ongoing support is included

**Rule:** If a detail has not been confirmed, do not invent it. Ask the client or leave a clearly marked placeholder.

### B. Proposed Approach

I will approach the project by first understanding the problem and defining what success should look like, before moving into design or implementation.

The exact activities will depend on the agreed scope.

1. **Discovery and alignment:** Review the brief, existing materials, user needs, business objectives, and constraints.
2. **Problem definition and planning:** Clarify priorities, identify assumptions, and establish the direction of the solution.
3. **Design and development of deliverables:** Create the agreed designs, assets, prototypes, or implementation.
4. **Review and refinement:** Collect feedback, resolve agreed issues, and refine the work within the defined revision scope.
5. **Final delivery and handoff:** Provide the agreed files, documentation, assets, or implementation and explain any relevant handoff requirements.

Research, usability testing, development, automation, and other specialist activities are included only when explicitly agreed.

## 4. Tailoring Guidance by Project Type

Do not use the same deliverables for every client. Adapt the proposal to the actual service being offered.

### UX/UI and Product Design

Potential deliverables, only when agreed:
- Discovery and requirements synthesis
- User journeys, information architecture, or task flows
- Wireframes and high-fidelity UI designs
- Interactive prototypes
- Design system components or documented design specifications
- Usability testing and findings
- Developer handoff and design QA

Clarify which research activities, testing participants, screens, states, breakpoints, and revisions are included.

### Website or E-commerce Design

Potential deliverables, only when agreed:
- Website structure and page inventory
- Responsive UX/UI design
- Product, collection, cart, or checkout experiences
- CMS or e-commerce configuration
- Content entry and asset preparation
- Integrations and analytics setup
- Testing, launch support, and handoff

Explicitly distinguish design from implementation. Clarify platform, page limits, product-entry limits, integrations, hosting, subscriptions, maintenance, and launch responsibilities.

### AI Solutions and Automation

Potential deliverables, only when agreed:
- Workflow and requirements analysis
- AI solution architecture or workflow mapping
- Prototype or proof of concept
- Prompt, agent, or automation configuration
- Integration with specified tools or systems
- Evaluation, testing, and documented limitations
- Deployment support and handoff documentation

Define the boundaries of the solution, supported systems, data access, human approval requirements, expected failure handling, usage costs, and what is not guaranteed. Do not promise accuracy, autonomous performance, or business results without appropriate evidence.

### Graphic Design and Branding

Potential deliverables, only when agreed:
- Creative direction and visual exploration
- Selected design concepts
- Final approved artwork
- Exported files and agreed formats
- Brand guidelines or usage documentation

Clarify the number of concepts, revision rounds, file formats, source files, printing requirements, font and stock licences, and usage rights.

### Ongoing Design or Product Support

Potential deliverables, only when agreed:
- Defined monthly hours or capacity
- Prioritized task backlog
- Regular review meetings
- Agreed reporting or documentation
- Response and availability expectations

Define the billing period, capacity limits, rollover policy, response times, and how additional work is approved.

## 5. Final Pre-Send Quality Check

Before sending any proposal, verify:

- [ ] The client's name, company, project, and problem are accurate.
- [ ] The proposed outcome reflects the client's actual needs.
- [ ] Every deliverable is specific enough to understand and evaluate.
- [ ] Scope boundaries and exclusions are clear.
- [ ] Fees, currency, taxes, payment terms, and schedule are confirmed.
- [ ] Revision limits and approval responsibilities are explicit.
- [ ] Ownership, confidentiality, and portfolio permissions are not assumed.
- [ ] No unsupported metrics, testimonials, credentials, or promised outcomes have been added.
- [ ] The proposal uses professional, client-specific language rather than generic filler.
- [ ] Any unresolved terms have been clarified or explicitly left open.
- [ ] The final version has been reviewed and approved before it is sent.

## 6. Instructions for Codex

When using this file to prepare a proposal:

1. Read the relevant project brief, client notes, and approved positioning materials before drafting.
2. Extract only facts supported by the supplied materials or explicitly confirmed by the user.
3. Preserve the distinction between confirmed facts, assumptions, recommendations, and open questions.
4. Do not invent client names, requirements, deliverables, prices, timelines, metrics, credentials, legal terms, or commitments.
5. If essential information is missing, ask focused questions before producing a final client-ready proposal.
6. When some details remain unresolved, use clear placeholders and provide a separate internal checklist titled **“Hasina — Confirm Before Sending.”**
7. Tailor the scope and deliverables to the specific engagement; do not include every service listed in this template by default.
8. Keep the positioning product-minded and user-centered: connect business needs, user needs, problem-solving, and thoughtful design.
9. Use concise, confident, professional language. Avoid exaggerated claims, unnecessary jargon, and generic AI-generated phrasing.
10. Never send the proposal, contact the client, accept terms, or make a commercial commitment without explicit authorization.
11. Before finalizing, check the proposal against the pre-send quality checklist above.

**Guiding principle:** A strong proposal is not a long list of services. It is a clear agreement about the problem, the value to be delivered, the work included, and how both parties will collaborate successfully.
