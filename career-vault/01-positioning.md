# Professional Positioning — Hasina Parvin

## Purpose

This is the source of truth for how I explain my professional identity across my CV, LinkedIn, portfolio, interviews, proposals, and design collaboration with Codex.

Positioning should be consistent without making every channel sound identical. Adapt the length and emphasis to the audience, but preserve the same core identity and evidence standards.

## 1. Core positioning

**Primary role: Product Designer / UX Designer**

I am a product-minded UX/UI Designer who connects customer understanding, business needs, and design execution to shape clear, useful digital experiences. My work spans problem understanding, research contribution, product and feature decisions, interaction and visual design, collaboration with developers, and learning from feedback. I am especially interested in eCommerce and AI-powered digital products.

**Short positioning line**

> I turn customer needs and product challenges into thoughtful digital experiences by connecting research, product thinking, and visual design.

**Guiding principle**

> Don't start with the screen. Understand the problem, make thoughtful product decisions, and design an experience that creates real value.

**Long-term direction**

Grow into an exceptional Product Experience Designer who can frame ambiguous problems, make evidence-informed product decisions, craft distinctive and accessible experiences, and help shape AI-native products responsibly.

This is an ambition and development direction—not a claim that every capability has already been proven at expert level.

## 2. What makes my perspective distinctive

My differentiator is the combination of:

- **Customer and business understanding:** experience working with customers, understanding business needs, and translating conversations into actionable requirements.
- **UX and product thinking:** attention to user problems, journeys, feature decisions, and the relationship between a product's value and how people use it.
- **Design craft:** interaction, interface, visual hierarchy, and clear communication—not aesthetics without a reason.
- **Technology collaboration:** working with developers to understand feasibility and deliver usable experiences.
- **AI-enabled product interest:** exploring how AI, automation, and agentic workflows can make products more useful, while keeping user needs, transparency, and control central.

The narrative is: **I understand the product from both sides—the customer/business side and the design/technology side.**

Use this as a differentiator, not as a reason to overstate technical, research, or AI expertise.

## 3. Target audiences and emphasis

### European employers and hiring teams

Lead with the recognizable role **Product Designer** or **UX Designer / Product Designer**. Show balanced capability across problem framing, research contribution, product thinking, interaction design, visual design, collaboration, and iteration. Use specific case studies to prove the scope and depth of the work.

Target suitable mid-level roles and roles with room to grow toward senior-level responsibility. Do not label me Senior or claim senior-level scope unless the vacancy requirements and evidence support it.

### Freelance clients and business stakeholders

Emphasize the ability to understand the business challenge, clarify what customers need, structure a practical solution, and collaborate toward a coherent digital product—not simply deliver attractive screens.

Explain outcomes in terms the client can understand. Only use quantified business results when a reliable source supports them.

### Product/design and AI community

Share practical thinking about UX, product decisions, visual craft, and AI-powered experiences. Explain why a decision was made, what trade-off it involved, and what evidence would validate it. Avoid tool hype and unsupported claims of expertise.

## 4. Evidence to build the positioning around

### eBitans — primary professional case study

eBitans is an AI-assisted/eCommerce website-builder product for small business owners. My experience included work across much of the website-building experience, customer conversations and research contribution, identifying pain points, defining or shaping features and design decisions, collaborating with developers, and learning from post-launch feedback.

The story should show how I helped make website creation more accessible to non-technical business owners and reduced the need to depend on developers for routine setup and management.

**Evidence to assemble before publishing:** precise dates and role scope; examples of research activities and decisions; screens or flows I personally designed; collaboration boundaries; available customer feedback; and verified product or business outcomes. Do not conflate eBitans with eCommerce X; they are separate products.

### Other experience

Earlier business development, customer-facing, and digital-services experience can support the customer/business side of my perspective. Use the exact employers, titles, dates, and duration from verified career records rather than combining approximate totals into an unsupported claim.

### Portfolio concepts and AI experiments

Styra, Leafora, e-bike work, and other concepts can demonstrate product thinking, design craft, prototyping, and AI-assisted workflows when their status is clearly labelled. Distinguish launched products and client work from self-initiated concepts, prototypes, and explorations.

Personalize this narrative for each interview using only verified examples and the actual responsibilities I held.

## 6. Messaging pillars

1. **Problem before interface** — clarify the real user need and desired outcome before choosing a screen or pattern.
2. **Evidence-informed decisions** — connect observations, requirements, and hypotheses to design choices; identify what still needs validation.
3. **Business and human value together** — understand customer goals and business constraints without letting one erase the other.
4. **Thoughtful design craft** — clear hierarchy, purposeful visual choices, accessible interactions, and context-specific design.
5. **Responsible AI-enabled experiences** — use AI where it meaningfully helps people; preserve clarity, trust, user control, and honest communication.
6. **Learning through iteration** — prototype, test where possible, learn from feedback, and explain what changed and why.

## 7. How Codex should use this positioning

When helping with my portfolio, case studies, CV, LinkedIn, interviews, proposals, or product-design work:

- Read this file alongside `principles/design-taste.md`, `principles/anti-ai-slop.md`, and `principles/codex-contract.md` when relevant.
- Keep the role identity consistent: Product Designer / UX Designer, with customer/business understanding and interest in AI-powered products as differentiators.
- Start with the user problem and product context, not with visual styling or AI tools.
- Explain consequential decisions, rationale, alternatives, and trade-offs.
- Separate verified facts, direct observations, hypotheses, and unknowns.
- Never invent research, metrics, customer quotes, project outcomes, responsibilities, dates, credentials, or tool proficiency.
- Clearly label launched work, client work, internal product work, and self-initiated concepts.
- Tailor language to the audience without changing the underlying truth.
- Prefer concrete evidence and a clear story over buzzwords, inflated seniority, or generic personal-brand claims.
- When information is missing, ask for it or mark it as needing verification; do not silently fill the gap.

## 8. Claims that need verification before external use

- Exact total years of experience and the split between business development and UX/UI/product design.
- Exact role titles, dates, employment relationships, and ownership boundaries.
- The precise research methods used and whether each activity was led, shared, or supported.
- Product metrics, customer counts, conversion, revenue, retention, and impact claims.
- The extent of AI/automation implementation versus experimentation, learning, or concept work.

Until verified, use careful language such as “contributed to,” “worked across,” or “explored” where accurate.

## 9. Ongoing development priorities

- Strengthen research planning, synthesis, and traceability from evidence to decisions.
- Build case studies that make the problem, constraints, alternatives, decision logic, trade-offs, and validation visible.
- Improve prototyping, usability testing, accessibility, design systems, and developer handoff.
- Develop a credible body of work in AI-powered product experiences, including trust, transparency, recovery, and user control.
- Build a consistent professional presence that demonstrates thinking through useful examples rather than only visual showcases.

## 10. Positioning quality check

Before publishing any professional statement, ask:

- Is the role immediately understandable to the intended audience?
- Is the differentiator specific and credible?
- Does the statement connect to real work or a clearly labelled learning project?
- Does it show how I think, not only what tools I use?
- Are all claims supportable?
- Does the language sound like a thoughtful designer rather than generic AI-generated branding?
- Does it leave room for growth without pretending I have already reached the destination?

**Positioning standard:** premium in appearance, clear in communication, credible in evidence.
