## First post — a rejected assumption from the eBitans case study

**The problem wasn't that customers needed more instructions. It was that the workflow didn't scale.**

One lesson from my work on eBitans changed how I think about product design.

Initially, customers had to upload products to their online stores one by one.

Our first instinct could have been to provide more guidance or tutorials. But while talking with customers, I learned that some businesses managed hundreds—or even thousands—of products. Many already had their product names, descriptions, and images organized together.

The real problem wasn't knowing *how* to upload a product.

It was repeating the same process hundreds of times.

So I helped shape the direction for a spreadsheet-based bulk-upload feature, worked with the team to define the requirements, and discussed feasibility with developers.

The team delivered the feature, and customers appreciated the easier upload process.

The lesson I took away:

**Before improving a screen, question the workflow behind it.**

Sometimes users don't need better instructions. They need less unnecessary work.

That is the kind of product designer I want to keep becoming: someone who listens carefully, challenges assumptions, and helps turn real customer problems into practical product decisions.
